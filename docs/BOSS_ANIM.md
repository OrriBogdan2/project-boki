# Boss animation (scripts/match/boss_anim.gd)

Each lair boss (colossus, serpent, brood) has keyed moves for every state: sleep, wake, idle, walk, stagger, phase change and death, plus a `<attack>_windup` and `<attack>_strike` for each attack. `Boss.render` calls `BossAnim.pose()` once a frame.

- **Wind-up = the tell.** A wind-up move runs 0..1 over `BossAnim.hit_t(boss)`, the seconds the ground telegraph takes to fill, so the body has gathered the frame the tell is full. The strike move starts on that frame (a step in `Boss.steps` fires the blow there). `--boss-anim-check` asserts both against the live attack code, enraged and not.
- **Overhead camera.** The match camera looks almost straight down, so the big moves also turn (`ry`), swell (`s`) and shift (`pz`); lean and rise alone barely show.
- **Legendary Boki.** A lair holds a legendary Boki: colossus = Siegephant, serpent = Sarcobra, brood = Calderoise. Each is the dex's approved model, skinned to the pivots its moves pose (`art/mobs/<kind>/<kind>.glb`, one bone per pivot; Siegephant also has `LegFL/FR/BL/BR`, walking in diagonal pairs). `MobGlb` gives each bone a bare pivot and copies the pivots onto the bones (docs/art/MOB_MODELS.md). They keep their own paint (no region repaint). Remake one with `boss_skin.py` (Blender, in /home/boki/meshy/boss-legendaries-1114/tools): `--kind <kind> --mesh <dex glb> --height <m>`; 9000 triangles at most.
- **Primitive bodies** (`--prim-art`, or no model): the Colossus is a rigged pack model whose moves also pick the clip and which stretch plays (`CLIP`); the wind-up stops on the clip's own hit frame. Serpent and brood are posed pivots; the serpent's coils still follow `Boss.trail`. A pivot only one body has is skipped on the other.
- **Death** runs `DEATH_S`, then the body holds its last pose until `Boss.DEATH_FADE_S` (the shared dissolve, ext/vfx-mob-death, replaces that fade when merged).
- **Online (Net 0.24.6).** The host sends one `bat` event (`scripts/match/boss_net.gd`) when a boss's attack, how long it has run, phase, asleep/awake, enraged or wind-up seconds change: about 32 bytes, two per attack (start, end), so roughly 20 B/s for a boss in a fight and nothing asleep. A remote game builds a real `Boss` (not a plain `Mob`), takes the event, and counts `atk_t` on from the synced age (the match clocks of two games drift up to 0.5 s apart, so a start time would show the strike late), so `Boss.render` poses the wind-up, strike, sleep, wake and phase as the host does. Only the loops (idle, walk, stagger) still follow the snapshot.
- Checks: `--boss-anim-check`; `--remote-anim-check` (the boss rows, 2 games over the relay); GPU strips: `--boss-anim-shots --boss=<id> --shots-dir=<dir> [--reel-frames]` through `jobq submit --needs gpu` (`--reel-frames`: frames for a clip, `<dir>/reel_<id>/`).
